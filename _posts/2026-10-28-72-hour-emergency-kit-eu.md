Since the European Commission recommended in 2025 that every household should be able to **manage on its own for 72 hours**, many people have been wondering what exactly they need. The answer is simpler than it seems. You do not need a bunker or strange gadgets: just gather in one place what you would need if you were left without power, tap water or the ability to leave home for three days.

## What the 72-hour kit is and why it is recommended

It is a supply that lets your family be **self-sufficient for three days** in an emergency: a long blackout, a storm, a flood or a supply disruption. In a large emergency, the emergency services have to prioritise people in danger. If other households can look after themselves, help reaches those who need it most sooner.

In March 2025 the European Commission presented its **Preparedness Union Strategy**, which encourages people to keep essential supplies for at least 72 hours. It is a self-protection recommendation, not an obligation.

## Full 72-hour emergency kit checklist

### Water and food
- **Water:** at least **2 litres per person per day** just for drinking, that is 6 litres per person for three days. If you can, keep a bit more for cooking and washing. We work it out in detail in [how much water and food you need for 72 hours](/en/blog/how-much-water-food-72-hours/).
- **Food that needs no cooking or refrigeration:** ring-pull tins, jars of cooked pulses, nuts, biscuits, bars or emergency rations.

### Light and information
- **A torch** and, ideally, a **lantern** that lights the whole room. Avoid candles: they cause fires.
- **Spare batteries** in the sizes your devices use.
- **A battery, hand-crank or solar radio.** If the power and internet go down, it is the most reliable way to receive official instructions.

### Power and communication
- **A charged power bank** for your phone (20,000 mAh gives two or three charges).
- **Charging cables.**
- **A list of important phone numbers on paper**, in case your phone runs out of battery.

### Health and hygiene
- **First aid kit.**
- **Regular medication** for several days and copies of prescriptions.
- **Thermal blankets**, light and very warm.
- Basic hygiene: wet wipes, hand sanitiser, bin bags and toilet paper.

### Money and documents
- **Cash in small notes.** Without power, card readers and cash machines do not work.
- **Copies of your ID, health card, insurance and property deeds** in a waterproof pouch.

### If your household includes…
- **Babies:** ready-to-feed formula, nappies and wipes.
- **Pets:** food and water for three days, and a lead or carrier.
- **Older or dependent people:** their medication, spare glasses or hearing aids and, if they use electrical devices, a backup battery plan.

## Home kit, car kit and go bag: not the same thing

| Kit | What it is for | Where to keep it |
|---|---|---|
| **Home kit** | Staying at home for three days without power, water or supplies | A box in a cupboard |
| **Car kit** | A night stuck on the road because of snow, rain or a traffic jam | A bag in the car boot |
| **Go bag** | Leaving home within minutes if the authorities ask you to | One backpack per person, by the door |

For most families the most useful is the **home kit**, because a blackout is the most likely emergency. We explain it in [what to do in a power outage](/en/blog/power-outage-guide-72-hours/). If you drive in winter or live in an at-risk area, add the [car kit](/en/blog/car-emergency-kit-spain/) and the [go bag](/en/blog/go-bag-checklist/).

## What you don't need

- **Buying everything at once.** Start with water, light, a radio and a battery; add the rest little by little.
- **Expensive "survival" products.** Most things in the kit are ordinary: torch, batteries, tinned food.
- **Stockpiling food for months.** The goal is three days, not a year.

## How to keep it up to date

1. **Once a year**, check the batteries, torch, power bank and that the cash is still there.
2. **Check the dates** on medication, food and water. Emergency water and rations usually last 5 years.
3. **Adjust the kit** when your household changes: a baby, a pet or an older relative moving in.
