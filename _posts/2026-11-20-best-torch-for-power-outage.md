When the power goes, the first thing you look for is a torch. And that is when you find out whether yours works, whether it has batteries and whether anyone knows where it is. In a long outage, one torch is not enough either: you need light to move around and light to stay in.

## The three kinds of light for a power outage

| Type | What it is for | Why we include it |
|---|---|---|
| **Handheld torch** | Moving around the house, checking the fuse box | Bright and easy to use |
| **Head torch** | Doing things hands-free: cooking, caring for someone, using stairs | The most practical in a backpack |
| **LED lantern** | Lighting a whole room | Replaces candles with no fire risk |

**Our recommendation:** at home, a **lantern** for the room where you will be and a **torch** per floor. In the go bag, a **head torch** per person.

## What to look for

### Standard batteries rather than built-in battery

A rechargeable torch is convenient day to day, but in an outage it only works if you charged it beforehand. With **AA or AAA batteries**, which you can buy anywhere, you just need a spare pack in your kit.

### Lumens: you don't need that much power

To move around the house, **100 to 300 lumens** is enough. Torches with thousands of lumens drain batteries quickly and dazzle in enclosed spaces.

### Low mode

This is what extends battery life the most. In an outage lasting hours, soft light for a long time is worth more than bright light for a short time.

### Toughness

If you will also carry it in the car or the backpack, look for a torch that resists knocks and splashes.

## Tips so it doesn't let you down

- **A torch in a fixed place on each floor**, and make sure the whole family knows where.
- **A spare pack of batteries** in the kit.
- **Check them once a year**, when you check the rest of the kit.
