The blackout of 28 April 2025 caught almost everyone in Spain unprepared. The good news is that most of what makes a difference can be done **in half an hour**, without spending much or filling your home with stuff.

This guide covers **before and after** a power outage. What to do while it lasts is explained in [what to do in a power outage: a guide to the first 72 hours](/en/blog/power-outage-guide-72-hours/).

## What you can do today in 30 minutes

1. **Find your fuse box** and learn how to switch the main breaker and the trip switches back on. That way you can tell whether the outage is only in your home.
2. **Keep a torch in a fixed place** on each floor (the hallway, the kitchen, the bedside table) and check it has batteries. Make sure everyone at home knows where it is.
3. **Charge your power bank** and keep it with your phone charger. If you do not have one, it is the first thing worth buying.
4. **Try the manual release** on your garage door and on any motorised shutters or awnings.
5. **Take out some cash** in small notes and keep it with a copy of your important documents.
6. **Write down important numbers on paper**: family, doctor, your electricity company and 112, the emergency number in Spain and across Europe.
7. **Fill the gaps in your freezer** with bottles of water. Frozen, they help everything stay cold for longer if the power goes, and afterwards they are drinking water.

> If someone at home depends on an electrical device (oxygen, a sleep apnoea machine, an electric wheelchair), talk to their doctor or the supplier about a backup battery and a plan.

## What to keep at home at all times

| For | What | How much |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Battery LED torch or lantern | One per floor |
| Information | Battery, hand-crank or solar radio | One |
| Power | 20,000 mAh power bank | One per 2 people |
| Water | Bottled water or water pouches | 6 litres per person |
| Food | Food that needs no cooking | For 3 days |
| Money | Cash in small notes | €150–200 |
| Health | First aid kit and regular medication | For several days |

To work out water and food for your household, there is a table in [how much water and food you need for 72 hours](/en/blog/how-much-water-food-72-hours/).

## When the power comes back

1. **Wait a few minutes** before plugging anything in: there can be power surges when the electricity returns.
2. **Plug appliances back in one at a time**, starting with the fridge and freezer. If you connect everything at once, the trip switch may go again.
3. **Check your food.** If anything has been above 4 °C for more than two hours (meat, fish, dairy, leftovers), throw it away. If in doubt, don't risk it.
4. **Restart your router** and reset clocks, ovens and alarms.
5. **Replace what you used**: batteries, water, food and the power bank charge. It is the step people forget most, and the one that leaves you with nothing for the next outage.

## The most dangerous mistakes

- **Using candles.** Every blackout causes house fires. A torch is cheap and burns nothing.
- **Running a generator, a gas stove or a barbecue indoors**, even on an enclosed balcony or in the garage. They produce **carbon monoxide**, which you cannot see or smell and which can kill in an enclosed space.
- **Opening the fridge every so often.** Every time you open it, it loses cold.
- **Draining your phone battery** on videos or social media. You may need it for an emergency.
- **Calling 112 to ask when the power will be back.** It overloads the line people need for real emergencies. Use the radio for information.

## A kit in a box, then forget about it

The most practical option is to keep everything together in a box in a cupboard: light, radio, battery, water, food, cash and documents. That way you do not have to look for anything in the dark, and you only need to check it once a year.
