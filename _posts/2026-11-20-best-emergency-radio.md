During the blackout of 28 April 2025 in Spain, many people had no idea what was going on: without power there was no wifi, and in many areas phones lost signal within a few hours. People with a battery radio knew what was happening and what to do.

That is why a radio is one of the most important items in a home emergency kit. But not just any radio will do: the kitchen radio that plugs into the wall is useless without power.

## What to look for in an emergency radio

### 1. How it is powered

This is the most important point. The more ways it can run, the better:

| Power | Advantage | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| **Batteries** | Hours of listening with no effort | They run out; you need spares |
| **Rechargeable battery** | Convenient day to day | Useless if you did not charge it beforehand |
| **Hand crank (dynamo)** | Never runs out | You have to crank it every so often |
| **Solar panel** | Charges itself in daylight | Slow, and no use at night |

**Our recommendation:** a radio that combines **hand crank, solar and a battery or batteries**. That way you always have a way to turn it on.

### 2. AM and FM bands

FM sounds better, but AM reaches further. In an emergency it is worth having both, in case a station stops broadcasting.

### 3. Torch and USB output

Many emergency radios include a **torch** and a **USB port** to give your phone some charge. These are useful extras, although the crank gives you a short call, not a full phone charge.

### 4. Size and toughness

For home use, size matters little. If you also want it in your go bag, look for a small one that is splash-resistant.

## Tips so it works when you need it

- **Charge and test it once a year**, when you check your kit.
- **Write down the frequencies** of your local stations in AM and FM on paper.
- **Keep it next to the torch and batteries**, somewhere the whole family knows.
