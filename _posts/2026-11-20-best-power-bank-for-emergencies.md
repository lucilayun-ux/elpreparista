In an emergency, your phone is your backup torch, your way to reach your family and your access to information. And in a power outage, it runs out. A charged power bank is probably the purchase that makes the biggest difference in an emergency kit.

## How many mAh you need

| Capacity | Real phone charges | What for |
|---|---|---|
| 10,000 mAh | 1 to 2 | Everyday use, a short outage |
| **20,000 mAh** | **2 to 3** | **A long outage or the car: our recommendation** |
| 26,800 mAh or more | 4 or more | Several people, but quite heavy |

Real charges are fewer than the mAh suggest, because some energy is lost in conversion. That is why we plan **one 20,000 mAh power bank for every two people**.

## What else to look for

### Fast charging over USB-C

A **fast-charging USB-C output** (for example, 20 W or more) charges your phone in much less time, which matters when you don't know how long the outage will last.

### Several outputs

With two or more ports you can charge two devices at once: two phones, or the phone and the radio.

### Charge display

Seeing the exact percentage helps you share out the power and know when to recharge it at home.

### Weight

A 20,000 mAh power bank weighs around 300 to 400 grams. For a go bag, it is not worth going heavier.

## Tips so it's ready

- **Keep it charged** and top it up **every three to six months**.
- **Store it with its cable** and your phone charger.
- **During the outage, save power:** battery saver mode on your phone, low brightness and messages instead of calls.
